# Write a shot

Describe one visible action with a clear beginning and ending. Name the character or prop with an
`@mention`, state what changes, and give camera direction only when it matters.

> @Maya puts @RedCup on the counter, looks toward the window, and steps out of frame. Hold a medium
> shot from the doorway. Keep the morning light and room layout from @Kitchen.

Keep reference notes about stable appearance. Put the current action in the shot prompt. Location
references should describe layout, time of day, and lighting. Avoid embedding future story events
inside a character's reference notes.

Use **New Scene** with a clean starting image when changing locations or removing subjects. A person
already visible in an inherited image can remain visible even after their reference is deselected.

## Dialogue

Choose the film's intended language explicitly. Give each line a stable speaker and a timing window.
Keep lines short enough for their assigned duration. Mark shots without dialogue accordingly.
Review generated speech by listening; prompts alone do not guarantee the exact words or language.
Connect reviewed authored audio when exact speech is required.

## Continuity

Select **Keep this detail** only after reviewing its visual evidence. Reuse approved evidence for
identity or state that must remain consistent. Use **Must remain visible throughout** only when
exits, occlusion, and cutaways are forbidden.

Keep seeds fixed while comparing prompt or reference changes. Change one thing at a time so you can
judge its effect. Review the full motion, not just the first and last frames.
